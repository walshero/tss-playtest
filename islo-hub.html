The iSLO Suite. Games that build the skill — and the instrument that measures it.

MassBay asks every graduate to become proficient in seven competencies. This is one station for each. Every station holds the games that build the skill by playing it, and the rubric that measures it against the college's own language.

Same skill, two instruments. All seven outcomes now have a build — the last dry cells (Natural World and DEI) were filled this pass. Where the measure side is still a rubric the college hasn't normed yet, this page says so out loud, marked, not hidden.

1

Graduation Competency 1

Written & Oral Communication

Graduates will explain, persuade, advocate, and argue effectively when engaged with a variety of audiences.

Built

Build it — play these

The Tell LiveRead the moment a text gives itself away. Ten craft moves, and the reader-test for each. Sandbags LiveCut the weight a sentence is carrying and see what it was hiding. Cliché Cowpaths LiveWorn language nobody chose and everybody walks. Three rooms: the city, the field, the line. Flash Ballast LiveWhat a very short story can and cannot carry. The Workshop Wall LivePeer review as a public act — the oral half of the outcome. What you say about a draft is a draft too. EN195 Course Hub LiveThe whole writing course, six rooms, where these moves are taught in sequence. Score the Room LiveThe oral half at last: read a described delivery — the words plus how they were said — and score it on the AAC&U Oral Communication rubric. Never captures audio; never judges accent or dialect. Whose Draft LiveWriting with AI, honestly. Walk one essay, decide at each step how much to hand to AI, and watch what stays yours and what a reader or rubric would catch. Ends with a disclosure statement you can actually use. Grounded in the English dept's own findings. iSLO 1 & 3.

Graduation Competency 2

Quantitative Skills

Graduates will use a variety of mathematical tools and quantitative reasoning to solve problems and analyze complex challenges.

Built

Build it — play these

The Tension Bar Client engineDecomposes a headline price into what it actually costs — a doorman is about $18,400 a year. The quantitative-reasoning engine the student build reuses. Warriors Fantasy Arcade LiveThe draft board as a numbers exercise — what the stat line shows, and what it leaves out. Sticker Price LiveFour headline numbers — a loan, a tuition sticker, a statistic in an argument, a small monthly charge — pulled apart into what they really cost over time. Guess first, then the arithmetic. Real amortization; net-price and denominator built in. Real Cost LiveThe same engine, for the lives students actually lead — the commute, the hours, the book, the whole bill. A small number multiplied by a real week. Dignity-first: your time and resourcefulness are assets on the ledger, and you set every number.

Measure it

Quantitative Literacy VALUE Not yet normed

The AAC&U Quantitative Literacy VALUE rubric is the field standard, but MassBay has not yet normed a local version. Proposed dimensions for the suite:

  • Interpretation — read a number in its context
  • Calculation — apply the right tool correctly
  • Assumptions — surface what the number hides
  • Communication — say what it means to a reader

Status: the build side now ships (Sticker Price, Real Cost); the measure side is still open. Norming a local QL rubric is a faculty task the suite can now support with real work samples.

Draft one to norm: Rubric Forge — start from the AAC&U QL scaffold and localize.

Graduation Competency 3

Technological, Computer & Information-Science Facility

Graduates will understand and use appropriately a variety of technological tools.

Built

Build it — play these

Flok LiveTake a growth job and pull the levers yourself — the streak, the batch, the feed. Then the machine shows you your own telemetry. Funny Boney's Factory In buildBuild the machine that makes the jokes — tools that build tools, and code that farms. Systems literacy by construction. Whose Draft LiveThe ethics half, applied to the tool of the moment: using AI on your writing appropriately and honestly. Decide what to hand off at each step, see the trade, and leave with a disclosure statement. Cross-listed with iSLO 1.

Behind these tools: How an Idea Travels — a guided read, not a game. One idea followed from the back of a napkin through the studio’s gates to a machine a stranger can play. How the tools on this shelf get made, and why most ideas don’t ship.

Measure it

Tool-use & systems insight Suite-proposed

No standalone MassBay rubric is normed for this outcome. The suite proposes a criterion set that fits what these games actually exercise:

  • Fluency — operate the tool to reach a goal
  • Systems insight — explain how it works on you
  • Appropriate use — choose the right tool, and the ethics of it

Note: Flok is deliberately a dark-patterns game. Reading the manipulation is the appropriate-use criterion — you cannot use a tool well until you can see what it is doing.

Draft one to norm: Rubric Forge — start from the AAC&U Information Literacy scaffold.

GenAI, appropriately used: the appropriate-use criterion now has a live pair — students in Whose Draft, faculty in AI-Resilient Assignment.

Graduation Competency 4

Knowledge About the Natural World

Graduates will use scientific knowledge and methodology to test, validate, and update their knowledge about the natural world.

Built

Build it — play this

Update the Model LiveHold a belief about the natural world, name in advance what would prove you wrong, meet real cited evidence, and update. Three beliefs (falling objects, the 10% brain, the full moon). The tight-spiral loop — hypothesis, test, revision — made playable.

Companion: The Viscosity models a scientific framework (Maslow) as a space — same reveal pattern, a different corner of the natural-world outcome.

Measure it

Scientific reasoning Not yet normed

MassBay has not normed a local rubric here. AAC&U's Inquiry & Analysis and Scientific Reasoning frameworks map cleanly onto the proposed mechanic:

  • Hypothesis — state a claim that could be wrong
  • Evidence — weigh what the data actually shows
  • Revision — update the belief, and say why

Status: the build side now ships (Update the Model); the measure side is open — no locally-normed MassBay rubric exists yet, so the game's criteria are suite-proposed.

Draft one to norm: Rubric Forge — start from the AAC&U Scientific Reasoning scaffold.

Measure it

Responsibility & self-assessment Suite-proposed

No normed MassBay rubric. Because this outcome is largely self-reported, the suite proposes a reflective, ipsative instrument rather than a scored artifact:

  • Responsibility — own the choice and its result
  • Self-assessment — locate yourself honestly
  • Collaboration — contribute to, and credit, the group
  • Civic awareness — see past the self to the community

Fit: these games already produce the reflection; the measure is a rubric for reading it back, not a test. Dignity-first: a starting point is never a verdict.

How to read a station. On the left: the games. You build the skill by running into it — no tutorial, no account. On the right: the measure — the actual MassBay or AAC&U rubric, reproduced verbatim, the same instrument faculty use to score the graduation portfolio.

The rubric is ipsative by design. A starting point is never a verdict. It measures where a piece of work sits against the college's levels — and, played again, against your own earlier draft. It is not a ranking against your classmates.

How the suite is scored

The measure is not bolted on afterward. It is the same instrument the college already uses on the graduation portfolio — and it obeys the studio's floor.

Verbatim rubrics

Where MassBay has a normed rubric — Written Communication, ISLO #5 — it is reproduced word for word, attributed and dated. A criterion nobody can trace is a rumour.

Ipsative, not ranked

You are measured against the college's levels and your own earlier draft, never against a classmate. Criterion-based grading is what keeps minoritized students in the room.

The gaps are named

One engine, seven faces

Every station is the same move — make the invisible constraint visible, let the student find it. The rubric names what was found. The game is the practice; the rubric is the mirror.
