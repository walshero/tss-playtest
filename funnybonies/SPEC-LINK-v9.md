# Funnybonies v9 - spec link and re-spine

Tight Spiral Studios · 2026-08-24 · Written before the build.

## Specs this build is made against

1. `studio/laughter-foundry-spec-and-log.html` (The Laughter Foundry, 2026-06-28) -
   the older and truer lineage. Founder's own words in that log:
   > *"Create a game that makes kids laugh and shows them that not everyone laughs at
   > the same things. Give them the tools to make pieces for a Rube Goldberg machine of
   > working parts, all designed to induce different types of laughter."*
   From it v9 takes: **the parts ARE laugh mechanisms**, the Busytown labeled cutaway
   (every part named, captioned, doing a visible job), tier-2 vocabulary, and Nunan's
   language-focus phase (name the thing after the kid has felt it).
2. `rescued/shelf-2026-07-13/funny-boneys-factory-spec.md` (GDD v2) - origin, floors,
   and the standing brief that the look is decided fresh per build.

## Audience

**Kids, roughly grades 3 to 6.** In the game, not at a kitchen table.

## Re-spine (PIVOT, founder-called 2026-08-24)

**Old spine (v8):** an instrument. The kid builds a machine out of real household
objects and the app only measures the gap between predicted and actual laughs.

**New spine:** **a built, lit, animated workshop where every part is a different kind of
funny, and the kid learns what each kind is by making it happen.**

**What it displaces:** v8 is KILLED. Founder ruling, twice: *"This is not the game."*
and *"Not in real life."* The real-world machine premise is void. The calibration
instrument is void.

**What survives:** the animation craft standards from `PRD-v7.md` section 8
(anticipation, the pause, overshoot, settle), the belt, the spec-first habit, and the
GDD floors. **No palette carries.** Nothing else.

## The onion (founder-authored 2026-08-24, drafted by Claude and corrected in chat)

Layers of funny, outer to inner. Outer layers land on nearly everyone; inner layers
split the room, and **that split is the lesson** the Foundry brief names.

1. The body - something falls over
2. **Surprise** - it does the thing you did not expect
3. **Again, but different** - repetition with variation
4. **Too much** - escalation, absurd excess
5. Wrongness - the thing in the wrong place
6. Rude - gross-out, the forbidden
7. Knowing - recognition, irony, the in-joke

**Founder call: build 4 and 2 first, and teach the player.** Layer 3 followed in v9.1.

## Scope - three parts, and the order between them

Named laugh-parts, one per built layer. Metaphorical names, tier-2 vocabulary, per the
Foundry's part-library convention.

| Part | Onion layer | Why it is funny (the taught line) |
|---|---|---|
| **THE ECHO** | 3, Again but different | Two the same makes a rule. The third one breaks it. Break it too early and there was never a rule. |
| **THE AVALANCHE** | 4, Too much | It does not know when to stop. |
| **THE JACK** | 2, Surprise | The waiting is the joke, not the popping. |

**v9.1 (2026-08-27) adds THE ECHO**, onion layer 3. It went in because layers 4 and 2 need
something to work against: escalation and surprise both want a rule to break, and with
three parts the ordering lesson stops being a coin flip and becomes a real shape.

**The mechanic is the ORDER.** Three parts, six orders, six different results, judged on
principle rather than from a lookup table: what it COSTS to open with a kind of funny,
and what it EARNS to end on one.

- Open on the Echo and everything after has a shape to sit in.
- Open on the Avalanche and it uses up most of the room straight away.
- Open on the Jack and the pop is only a noise, because nothing built to it.
- End on the Jack and the room goes quiet, then one small thing moves.
- End on the Avalanche and the flood buries whatever came before it.
- End on the Echo and nobody was counting any more, so the break had nothing to break.

**Echo, then Avalanche, then Jack** is the shape comedy usually wants: something to set
up, something to build, something small to finish it. The machine says so only after the
player has watched it happen.

No meter, no score, no oracle. Every order runs. None is punished.

## How it teaches (Nunan language focus, not a lecture)

1. The part sits in the workshop, unlabeled. The player taps it.
2. It performs alone, fully animated.
3. **Then** the name and the one line arrive. Vocabulary attached to a sensation the kid
   has already had.
4. The player sets the order, runs the whole chain, and the aftermath names the comedy
   result in plain words.

Never a score. Never "wrong." Every order runs; some land harder, and the game says why.

## Look - Samorost plus Lumino City, founder-called

A miniature that was **built and lit**, not drawn flat. Layered cardboard and cut paper
with visible edge thickness and grain, one warm practical light source casting real
shadow, three depth layers, ambient loops so the set breathes when nobody touches it,
and wordless causality.

**The v8 butcher-paper register is dead and does not carry.** Fresh palette: kraft
cardboard, cut-paper white, deep warm shadow, one lamp accent.

Both themes are diegetic: **light = the shutters are open**, **dark = the work lamp at
night.** Same room, two times of day.

## Floors (unchanged, all binding)

No emoji. No green in any structural role. High contrast both themes. 44px targets.
20px type, 18px absolute floor. Reduced motion gets a complete still-state telling.
Single file, offline, in memory only. No em dashes. Version chip beside the title,
matching `<title>` and the build banner.

## Out of scope

Onion layers 1, 5, 6, 7. More than three parts. Scoring, sharing, stacking, the
real-world-problem layer, the dashboards, any measurement instrument.

## Acceptance

1. Belt clean, all 11 ticks.
2. A kid can learn every part name and reason without an adult reading to them.
3. Every order runs. None is punished.
4. Every outcome is legible from a screenshot with the text hidden.
