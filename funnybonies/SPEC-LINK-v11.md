# Funnybonies v11 - spec link and re-spine

Tight Spiral Studios · 2026-08-29 · Written before the build.

## Specs this build is made against

1. `rescued/shelf-2026-07-13/funny-boneys-factory-spec.md` (GDD v2) - origin, floors, and
   the standing brief that the look is decided fresh per build. Origin, Peter at
   FableVision: **"a game for kids that makes them laugh."**
2. `studio/laughter-foundry-spec-and-log.html` - the laughter types. v11 keeps them but
   stops treating them as content. They are now **situations**, not vocabulary.

## Audience

**Kids, roughly grades 3 to 6.** On a phone, sound off.

## Re-spine (founder-called 2026-08-29)

Founder's ruling on v10:

> *"Funnybonies isn't quite intuitive enough, and it's not funny. I know we are building
> a Goldberg machine, but for now we need to achieve funny first."*
> *"It's a legit rotate and place and change view puzzle game with a funny lose state and
> a funny win state."*

**What this displaces.** The Rube Goldberg machine is not cancelled, it is **postponed**.
v10's bench of parts is shelved until the studio can make a thing that is actually funny.
Chasing the machine before the laugh produced three builds nobody laughed at.

**What is wrong with v10, stated plainly.** It is a demo, not a game. You press GO and
watch. There is no tension, nothing to get right, nothing to lose. Comedy needs
**jeopardy**, and a machine that always works has none. The parts were also illustrations
of jokes rather than jokes.

## The new spine

**A dessert tray, built like Jenga, at a restaurant that is losing its mind.**

Founder's frame: *"pretend we are playing a Jenga game where we are at a fancy restaurant
working to build a dessert tray."*

The player stacks cakes, macarons, jellies and sundaes on a round silver tray. It is a
real puzzle with a real fail state. **The comedy is not decoration on top of the puzzle,
it comes out of the puzzle**, because the two people watching you have opinions.

- **THE CHEF** wants it higher. Always higher. Every time you satisfy him he raises the
  bar again, physically, with his hand. He is delighted and unreasonable in equal parts.
- **THE WAITER** has to carry this. He grumbles. He gestures. He never speaks a word.
  Amanita register: everything he thinks is in his eyebrows, his shoulders and one
  exhausted arm.

## The three verbs

1. **ROTATE.** Every dessert has a footprint. Turning it 90 degrees swaps that footprint
   from wide to deep. A wide base is stable and eats space; a deep one is the opposite.
2. **PLACE.** Slide the piece along the axis you can currently see, then drop it. It
   lands with weight and a wobble.
3. **CHANGE VIEW.** **This is the puzzle.** The tray is round and you see it from ONE
   side. Turn the tray 90 degrees and you are looking down the other axis. A stack that
   looks perfectly centred from the front can be hanging off the back, and you will not
   know until you turn it. Every piece has a position on BOTH axes and you can only ever
   set one of them at a time.

Stability is honest, not scripted: the running centre of mass of everything above a level
must sit over the footprint below it. Drift too far and the tray goes.

## Where the funny comes from

The laughter types are not taught here and are never named on screen. They are **built
into the situation**, which is the overlap the founder asked for.

| Layer | How it shows up, natively |
|---|---|
| **Too much** | The chef's demand escalates every single round. The tray becomes absurd. |
| **Surprise** | The collapse. You never know which piece is the one that does it. |
| **Again but different** | The waiter's grumble is the same shape every round and gets a little worse each time. |
| **The body** | Everything falls over. That is the oldest joke there is. |

## The lose state, which has to be the best animation in the build

The tray does not simply fall. It goes in beats: **a lean, a hopeful pause where it might
hold, then everything goes at once.** Cream and jelly land on the waiter. He does not
flinch, he does not shout. He slowly wipes one eye, looks at the player, and holds the
look. The chef is thrilled.

## The win state

The chef gets his height and is briefly, genuinely happy. The waiter allows himself the
smallest possible moment of pride. Then the chef raises his hand higher. **Next round.**

## Look - a hard break from v10

**Colourful.** Founder's call: *"Start by going colorful not brown motif."* The kraft and
cocoa palette of v8 through v10 is dead and does not carry. Patisserie brights: raspberry,
lemon, apricot, cream, plum, blueberry, gold foil, with cocoa used only as a line colour
and never as a ground.

**No green in any role.** Standing accessibility floor, not negotiable, and it costs a
pistachio macaron. Worth it.

Character animation to the Amanita and Animata register: wordless, in the shoulders and
the eyebrows, with anticipation, a pause, overshoot and settle on every beat.

## Floors (all binding)

No emoji. No green. High contrast in both themes. 44px targets. 20px type, 18px absolute
floor. Reduced motion still resolves the round and tells the outcome. Single file,
offline, in memory only. No em dashes. **Sound is never a channel** (2026-08-28 floor).
Version chip and `.tsp-lab` chip beside the title.

## Out of scope

The Goldberg machine. The onion vocabulary on screen. Scoring beyond the chef's height
bar. Sharing, saving, dashboards, any measurement instrument.

## Acceptance

1. A kid works out rotate, place and turn without an adult reading anything.
2. Turning the tray reveals something you could not have known from the other view. If it
   does not, the third verb is decoration and must be cut.
3. The collapse is funny on the first viewing and still funny on the tenth.
4. Every gag reads with the sound off.
5. You can lose, and losing is the better animation.
