Real cost — the small number, at the size of a whole semester.

A trip, an hour, a book, a bill. Each one looks small on the day. Multiply it by a real life — commuting, working, parenting, learning in a second language — and it becomes something you can actually plan around.

You set every number. The page only multiplies, divides, and adds what you tell it — no claim about anyone's prices, and no verdict about you. Guess first, then watch it come apart.

Your real-cost reading

Four small numbers, four real ones.

Every one of these was already true about your life — the arithmetic just made it visible and plannable. Change any control and it re-reads: the numbers follow you, not the other way around.

The one move, four times

Take the number they show you. Ask what it really is — times how often, over how long, out of what whole. The answer is almost never the headline, and seeing it isn't bad news: it's the difference between a surprise and a plan. Your time, your resourcefulness, and the aid already working for you are assets on this ledger — name them, and the whole number stops being scary and starts being yours.
