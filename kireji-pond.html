Kireji Pond

蛙
切れ字の池Kireji Pond

Matsuo Bashō · 1686

Before the leap, stillness.

古池や
蛙飛び込む
水の音

furu ike ya
kawazu tobikomu
mizu no oto

old pond — ya
a frog jumps in
water's sound

Ya is a kireji, a cutting word. It marks the hinge: an old, quiet pond, then a frog, then a sound. The whole poem turns on that leap. (Word-by-word gloss.)

How to play

  • Phrases float down the stream. Tap one to stop it, then Catch it, Smash it, or Let it go.
  • Build three lines: 5 · 7 · 5 syllables. The dots under each phrase count them.
  • One line must turn: a leap, a splash, a sudden shift.
  • Spot a cliché? Tap it, then choose Smash cliché. A right call shatters it and clears the water. A wrong call, or catching a cliché, clouds it.
  • Rearrange anytime: move line 1 to the bottom (or 3 to the top), or remove any line.
  • Feed the frog three poems. Then listen.
