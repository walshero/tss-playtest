Six forms, one drawer

Every plate you pulled is in here. Read them together and the thing they have in common is not that they are false. It is that not one of them was written for the poem it was found in.

Where the word comes from. A cliché is a printing term before it is a criticism. French printers cast a whole common phrase as one solid plate, so it could be dropped into any form without setting the letters again, and named it for the sound the matrix made striking the metal. The English word for the same object is stereotype. That is the whole charge against a worn phrase: not that it is untrue, but that it arrived pre-cast, and would have fit anywhere.

All six poems are United States public domain, published between 1895 and 1923, and are set here exactly as published. The plates are written for this game. Nothing on this page is stored or sent anywhere. It runs in memory and forgets you when you close it.
