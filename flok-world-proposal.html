Flok: the world before the rebuild (proposal)

One sentence: Flok stops being a feed of strangers and becomes one woman's Tuesday night, from 10:52 PM to 12:09 AM. Every lever the player pulls lands on someone she actually knows.

What stays exactly as it is: the five levers, the operator's seat, the clock to midnight, the telemetry turn ("it measured you"), real photos of ordinary things, and Horvath's rule that the feed is pleasant-neutral, never eerie. The darkness comes from the mechanics, not the content.

1. Who is posting

Tess Navarro · @north_trail

  • Age 26. Dental hygienist at a clinic across town. First patient at 7:30 AM. That is the stake. Every minute past 11 costs her tomorrow, and the player knows it.
  • Moved back to her hometown last spring after a breakup. She walks the lake loop most evenings because it settles her.
  • Why she posts: her older sister Rosa moved to Denver two years ago and misses home. Tess started posting the lake for Rosa. The posts were a message to one person.
  • Profile: bio "lake loop most nights. back home." 214 followers, 301 following. 38 posts, almost all the lake.
  • What the game is about, in her terms: a private ritual (the walk, the photo for her sister) gets turned into a performance (the count). By midnight she is no longer posting for Rosa. She is posting for the number.

4. Rewards in more than one lane (your note)

Today lever 1 has one reward: a surprise like. Real apps pay out in at least six currencies, and they are scheduled variably across all of them.

Reward In Flok What it pulls on Research anchor
Approval Like counts Social reward Sherman et al. 2016, Psychological Science: likes activate reward circuitry in teens' brains
Recognition by a specific person "Rosa liked your photo." Mom's comment. Belonging Same study: who likes matters, not only how many
Curiosity / surveillance "Quinn viewed your story." The open loop Przybylski et al. 2013: fear of missing out
Novelty "Memories: 1 year ago." New follower. Suggested posts. Variable novelty Skinner 1953, variable ratio
Status / comparison tidepool's 2,341 vs your 4. "Better than 80% of your posts." Rank Social comparison
Progress Streak counts, "7 days in a row" Loss and sunk cost Kahneman and Tversky 1979 (already lever 3)
Obligation Tags, replies owed, Seen Reciprocity, guilt Cialdini (already lever 5)

Proposal for lever 1: instead of one Boost button, the operator gets four reward cards: a stranger's like, Mom's comment, Quinn's view and Rosa's like. Each pulls Tess back a different amount, and the amounts vary. The player discovers what the machine discovers: Quinn's view works better than Rosa's like. The reveal says it out loud.

5. Where the silence weighs (your note: Seen only lands if the relationships are real)

Lever 5 stops being "three messages" and becomes three people:

  • Priya (best friend, 64-day streak, "did you get my last one?"). Silence costs a friendship that is already being run by a streak.
  • Mom ("call me when you can"). Easy to ignore, which is the point.
  • Quinn ("lmk when you're free"). She has read it four times. Seen is the only reply she can bear to give, and the operator's switch makes even that visible.

The read-receipt switch now has a cost the player can feel: turning it on exposes Tess to all three, differently.

6. What the rebuild touches (scope, so you can size it)

  • Content: the cast, captions, DMs and the timeline above. The five levers keep their mechanics.
  • Lever 1: becomes four reward cards (new interaction; the biggest change).
  • Lever 2: the feed shifts from friends to strangers as you scroll (the proportion is the lesson).
  • Lever 3: the streak is with Priya, and the save is a photo of a ceiling.
  • Lever 4: the batch includes "Quinn viewed your story" and the Memories card.
  • Lever 5: three people, three weights.
  • The turn: Rosa's like at 12:06, buried. The reveal adds one line about what worked on Tess.
  • Photos: about 8 new ordinary photos through the commons-scout lane (a ceiling, a dog, mountains for Rosa, a band from far back, pots, a Memories photo of two coffee cups). No faces.
  • Checks: flok-feed-check gains the cast (each photo tied to its person); the senses run as usual.

7. Your calls before anything is built

  1. Tess. Keep the name, age, job and the 7:30 AM stake, or change them?
  2. Quinn, the ex. Include, or soften to a friend she's drifting from? The ex is more true to life and the stronger engine; the friend is gentler for a classroom.
  3. Lever 1 as four reward cards, or keep one button and vary the reward behind it? Cards give the player the discovery; one button is simpler.
  4. The 12:06 beat. Rosa's like arrives buried and Tess scrolls past. Keep, or let her see it?
  5. Order of work. I'd build in this order: cast and captions first (cheap, changes the feel the most), then levers 3 to 5 (content swaps), then lever 1's cards (new interaction), then the 12:06 turn.
