About this room, its rules, and the draft

You sit in a fiction workshop as an Asker. The writer, the Asked, sits at a round table with the class. The Advocate sits beside her and rules on every card you play. If a card misses, the Advocate asks you to reframe once. If it misses again, she calls on someone else. After a good question, she holds the silence so the Asked can think. The Recorder writes down every question and none of the answers, and the Asked takes that page home.

The roles and rules come from Jesse Ball, "The Asking," in Notes on My Dunce Cap. The ways of speaking come from Pat Schneider and the Amherst Writers & Artists method: say what is strong, say what stays with you, and speak of the narrator, never the writer. The cards come from Matt Walsh's handout "Mapping the Storyscape with Writerly Moves" and its Observation Tags, and every test line on a card is his.

The three drafts adapt stories by Matt Walsh: the opening of "Presto"; a passage from chapter one of Daiko, his novel set in Sasakami Village; and "Dead Ants," from Secret Worlds, his stories written to his friend Dan. Each is roughed down to read like a workshop draft, and the studio planted one flaw in each for practice. Art: original cut paper, CC0, Tight Spiral Paper Craft.
